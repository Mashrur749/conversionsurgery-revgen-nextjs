# Revenue Recovery System for Renovators

## Complete Business Reference Document

**Version 1.0 | May 3, 2026**

This document is the operating reference for launching and running a productized revenue recovery business for design-build renovation contractors. It consolidates the offer, ICP, pricing, delivery model, sales process, economics, compliance, and 90-day validation plan into one source of truth.

---

# 1. Executive Summary

The business sells a managed revenue recovery system to design-build renovation contractors. The system helps contractors capture missed calls, respond to new inquiries, follow up on outstanding estimates, recover stale opportunities, and see what is turning into booked consultations or closed files.

The core thesis is simple:

> Renovation contractors already pay for leads and spend time preparing estimates. The expensive leak is what happens after the lead comes in: slow response, inconsistent follow-up, stale quotes, and unclear source-to-job visibility.

The business does not sell websites, SEO, paid ads, or generic automation. It sells a narrow operational outcome:

> More booked consults from existing inquiries and outstanding estimates.

The first market is design-build renovation contractors in Calgary and Edmonton, especially kitchen/bath firms, basement renovators, whole-home renovators, and larger residential renovation firms doing roughly $1M-$10M in annual revenue.

The recommended launch path is:

1. Offer a free Estimate Recovery Audit through cold outreach.
2. Close the first 3 clients on Pilot pricing.
3. Deliver a tightly scoped Revenue Recovery System.
4. Use results to move to Standard pricing.
5. Expand select clients into Premium / Booked Estimate OS.

The core direction is viable. The important refinement is to begin with a Pilot tier, keep the offer simple publicly, and keep complex attribution/microsites as premium or add-on components rather than the cold outreach hook.

# 2. Strategic Decision

## 2.1 Go / Modify / No-Go

**Decision: Go, with modifications.**

The research supports the path: design-build renovation contractors have high project values, measurable lead leakage, and enough economic upside for a managed recovery system to be defensible. However, the first offer should not be overloaded with deep attribution, custom dashboards, or landing page work.

The launch offer should be focused on:

- missed-call capture
- new inquiry response
- estimate follow-up
- stale estimate reactivation
- basic dashboard and reporting
- booked consult rate improvement

Advanced attribution, CRM integration, and microsites should be reserved for the Premium tier or add-ons.

## 2.2 The Business We Are Building

This is a productized managed service business, not a SaaS startup and not a custom development agency.

The business sells implementation plus monthly operations. The setup fee pays for installation. The monthly fee pays for monitoring, optimization, reporting, support, and continuous recovery work.

## 2.3 What We Are Not Building

We are not building:

- a generic GoHighLevel agency
- a website design agency
- a paid ads agency
- an SEO agency
- a CRM replacement
- a custom software consultancy
- a lead-generation broker
- an AI receptionist commodity service

We are building a managed revenue recovery layer for renovation contractors.

# 5. Customer Problem Map

## 5.1 The Revenue Leak

The expensive leak sits between:

1. inquiry arrives
2. lead is acknowledged
3. consult is booked
4. estimate is sent
5. follow-up happens
6. homeowner responds or goes cold
7. job is won/lost
8. source and reason are recorded

Many contractors have tools, but the handoffs are weak. The business opportunity is not merely sending texts. It is installing and managing the operational layer that prevents warm opportunities from disappearing.

## 5.2 Pain Categories

| Pain | What the contractor feels | What we solve |
|---|---|---|
| Missed calls | Leads call while owner is on site | missed-call response and logging |
| Slow response | Homeowner contacts competitors | faster inquiry acknowledgment |
| Estimate ghosting | Quotes sit open with no systematic follow-up | estimate follow-up sequence |
| Stale opportunities | Old leads disappear in texts/spreadsheets | stale estimate reactivation |
| No visibility | Owner does not know what is open | dashboard and reporting |
| Unclear sources | Cannot connect source to booked work | source tracking in higher tiers |
| Admin overload | Follow-up depends on memory | managed system and escalation rules |

## 5.3 Primary Metric

The main metric to defend is:

> Booked consult rate from existing inquiries.

Definition:

> Consults booked within 14 days of inquiry divided by total qualified inquiries in that period.

This is better than promising closed revenue because it is closer to the system’s area of control. Closed revenue depends on pricing, sales skill, market conditions, project fit, and contractor execution.

The original all-in $1,000/month, first-month-free model is highly customer-friendly but weak for a new business because it gives away the hardest setup work before the customer has meaningfully committed.

The better business structure is:

> setup fee + 90-day managed term + month-to-month continuation.

This funds proper onboarding, filters serious clients, and protects fulfillment quality.

# 16. Compliance and Ethical Rules

## 16.1 Messaging Compliance

Because SMS and automated outreach are central to the service, compliance is not optional.

Must-have requirements:

- express consent capture where required
- unchecked opt-in checkbox on forms
- sender identification in messages
- STOP / unsubscribe handling
- consent record logging
- quiet hours / permitted-hours logic
- audit trail for messages
- A2P / 10DLC registration where required

## 16.2 Canadian Considerations

Canada’s CASL framework is stricter than many operators expect. Treat express consent as the operational standard for SMS workflows. The system should log consent source, timestamp, and opt-in language.

## 16.3 Data Privacy

Maintain privacy discipline:

- only store necessary lead data
- provide export on request
- define retention policy
- secure dashboard access
- avoid unnecessary sensitive notes
- disclose third-party processors when needed

## 16.4 Ethical / Halal-Aligned Boundaries

Do not build the system around:

- 0% APR / financing-first CTAs
- high-pressure urgency
- fake scarcity
- fake reviews
- misleading claims
- deceptive test leads

Use neutral CTAs like:

- “Book a design consultation”
- “Schedule a project feasibility call”
- “Get a fixed-scope estimate discussion”

# 19. Objection Handling

## 19.1 “We already have a CRM.”

Good. This does not replace your CRM. It makes sure the handoffs around the CRM actually happen: inquiry response, estimate follow-up, stale opportunity tracking, and reporting.

## 19.2 “We need more leads, not follow-up.”

Maybe. But if existing inquiries and estimates are not being followed up consistently, buying more traffic just scales the leak. This fixes what happens after the lead comes in.

## 19.3 “Can you guarantee revenue?”

No. We do not control your pricing, sales process, market, or close rate. We guarantee the system implementation and visibility: connected inquiries, flagged estimates, follow-up activity, and reporting.

## 19.4 “This sounds like software.”

It is managed service plus infrastructure. You do not learn software or manage dashboards. We set it up, run it, monitor it, and report back.

## 19.5 “Can we just do missed-call text-back?”

Missed-call text-back is only one piece. The bigger money usually sits in outstanding estimates and warm opportunities that go quiet.

## 19.6 “We are referral-driven.”

That can still be a strong fit. Referral-driven contractors still need estimate follow-up, review requests, payment reminders, and stale quote reactivation.
