# 1. Executive Decision

Decision: Proceed with the design-build renovation contractor niche and the revenue recovery offer stack, but launch with a pilot tier and keep the first sale narrower than the full technical vision.

| Field | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Go / modify / no-go | GO with modifications |
| Core offer family | Revenue Recovery System for design-build renovation contractors |
| First market | Calgary + Edmonton, then Vancouver / Toronto / selected US metros |
| First paid product | Pilot Revenue Recovery System at $3,500 setup + $1,500/month for first 3 clients |
| Standard product | Revenue Recovery System at $5,500 setup + $2,000/month |
| Premium product | Booked Estimate OS at $9,500 setup + $3,500/month |
| Primary proof metric | Booked consult rate from existing inquiries |
| Primary sales channel | Cold email + phone + Loom audit + warm intros |
| Biggest risk | Over-building technical attribution before proving simple booked-estimate recovery |
| Main mitigation | Lead with estimate and inquiry recovery; reserve attribution, microsites, and CRM depth for premium clients |

## What changed after research

- The core offer direction did not change: revenue recovery for renovation contractors remains the viable path.

- The first-client entry ramp changed: add a Pilot tier to reduce buying friction and obtain proof.

- The public offer should be simplified under one umbrella name: Revenue Recovery System for Renovators.

- The core metric should be booked consult rate, not vague pipeline value or guaranteed revenue.

- Technical attribution and microsites should remain premium or add-on work, not the first cold pitch.

- The 60-90 day $35k personal savings goal is possible only with annual prepays, warm intros, or fast closes; a more reliable base case is 5-7 months.

# 2. Business Thesis

Thesis: High-ticket renovation contractors do not only need more leads. Many already pay for leads, referrals, ads, directory profiles, or homeowner attention. The expensive leak happens after the lead arrives: slow response, missed calls, inconsistent estimate follow-up, stale quotes, no source visibility, and weak accountability between inquiry and booked consult.

| Business problem | Why it matters | Our response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New inquiries are not handled quickly | Homeowners often contact multiple contractors and may book the first credible responder. | Missed-call capture, inquiry response, internal alerts, and response workflows. |
| Estimates go cold | The contractor already spent time qualifying and quoting, but follow-up becomes manual and inconsistent. | Estimate follow-up sequences and stale estimate recovery. |
| Owner has no visibility | Leads, estimates, source, status, and follow-up history are scattered across calls, texts, inboxes, and CRM. | Pipeline dashboard, source/status tracking, and reports. |
| Software exists but is underused | Buildertrend, Houzz Pro, Jobber, and CRMs may have pieces, but contractors often lack implementation discipline. | Done-for-you managed system, not DIY software. |
| Generic agencies stop at lead generation | SEO/PPC agencies generate leads but rarely own mid-funnel conversion and attribution. | Revenue recovery layer between marketing and booked work. |

# 3. ICP and Qualification

## Primary ICP

| Field | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Niche | Design-build renovation contractors |
| Sub-niches | Kitchen/bath remodelers, basement renovators, whole-home remodelers, additions/interior renovation firms |
| Revenue range | $1M-$10M annual revenue |
| Team structure | Owner + at least one admin/coordinator + at least one estimator/salesperson |
| Average project value | $25k+ minimum; ideally $50k-$150k+ |
| Lead/estimate volume | 10-30+ monthly inquiries or 6+ estimates/month |
| Tech state | Uses Buildertrend, Houzz Pro, Jobber, JobNimbus, HubSpot, Pipedrive, spreadsheet, or messy CRM |
| Urgency signals | Runs ads, uses Houzz/Google/LSA, has stale estimates, admin overload, or owner-dependent follow-up |

## Best-fit signals

- 30+ Google reviews and visible project portfolio.

- Service pages for kitchens, baths, basements, additions, or whole-home work.

- Clear owner/operator, sales manager, or office manager contact.

- Evidence of active marketing: Google Ads, Local Services Ads, Houzz, Meta ads, BILD/RenoMark membership, or strong Google Business Profile.

- The company sends enough estimates that stale quotes are a real issue.

- The company has enough administrative maturity to flag estimates and mark won/lost outcomes.

## Avoid list

- Solo handyman operators or sub-$1M firms with no admin support.

- Low-ticket service contractors where one recovered job cannot justify the fee.

- Companies with no lead flow and no estimate volume.

- Companies wanting only SEO, ads, websites, or a full custom software build.

- Teams unwilling to connect forms, calendar, phone, CRM, or pipeline data.

- Contractors who primarily want financing CTAs or high-pressure sales tactics as the main conversion mechanism.

## Qualification scorecard

| --- | --- |

| Average project value? | $25k+ minimum; $50k+ preferred. |
| Monthly inquiries? | 10+ preferred; 15+ ideal. |
| Monthly estimates sent? | 6+ preferred. |
| Admin/coordinator present? | Yes; someone can help with onboarding and status updates. |
| Estimator/salesperson present? | Yes; at least one person owns consults and estimates. |

# 6. Detailed Offer Designs

## 6.1 Estimate Recovery Audit

| Field | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Role in stack | Free front-end diagnostic used in cold outreach and warm-intro conversations. |
| Price | Free. Optional future paid version: $250-$500. |
| Buyer-facing promise | I will show you where warm leads and estimates may be slipping through. |
| Best for | Cold prospects, warm intros, and prospects who need to see the problem before buying. |

### What we deliver

- 3-7 minute Loom audit or one-page teardown.

- Website inquiry path review: form, contact page, booking path, CTA clarity.

- Phone and missed-call path review using only ethical public-facing checks.

- Estimate follow-up diagnosis based on visible process gaps and discovery questions.

- Google Business Profile and review-response surface check.

- Simple leak map: inquiry, response, consult, estimate, follow-up, stale, won/lost.

- One recommended next step tied to the paid pilot.

### What is excluded

- Implementation work.

- Automation setup.

- CRM or dashboard configuration.

- Tracking code installation.

- Any promise of exact lost revenue.

### Success metrics

- Prospect agrees there is a follow-up or tracking leak.

- Prospect books a discovery call or asks what implementation would look like.

- Internal target: 8-15% reply rate on highly personalized outreach, 30%+ audit-to-call conversion once they view the audit.

### Delivery notes

- Keep each audit under 45 minutes of production time after the first few.

- Use a repeatable audit checklist to avoid over-customizing.

- Do not submit fake inquiries or create misleading test leads.

- Only audit what is public or what the prospect explicitly shares.

### Single-customer economics

- Usually not profitable by itself if free; it is a customer acquisition asset.

- Cost should be controlled to roughly 30-45 minutes of founder time.

- If audit creation exceeds 90 minutes per prospect, switch to a templated scorecard and reserve Looms for warm replies.

### Upgrade path

- Primary CTA is the Pilot Revenue Recovery System.

- If prospect is not ready, offer to revisit before fall / spring pipeline planning.

- 4-6 touch estimate follow-up sequence over 14-30 days.

- Stale estimate reactivation for a small initial batch of old quotes or unresponsive prospects.

- Basic pipeline: new inquiry, contacted, consult booked, estimate sent, follow-up active, stale, won, lost.

- Landing page or website rebuild.

- Paid ad management, SEO, social media, or content marketing.

- Human sales calling or live receptionist service.

- Unlimited custom workflow requests.

### Success metrics

- Booked consult rate from existing inquiries is baselined and visible.

- 80%+ of connected/eligible inquiries are logged with source and response status by day 30.

- Flagged estimates enter follow-up with visible status.

- Hot replies are escalated to the client.

- At least one credible recovery event or follow-up-generated conversation occurs during the 90-day period, when volume allows.

### Delivery notes

- This is a proof-building tier, not a permanent discount for all future clients.

- Position as limited pilot pricing in exchange for feedback and permission to use anonymized results.

### Upgrade path

- After 90 days, upgrade to Standard at $2,000/month if results and usage justify it.

- For the first one or two case-study clients, you may let them keep $1,500/month if they provide a strong testimonial and referral access.

# 7. Pricing Rules and Packaging

- Never stack monthly tiers. A customer pays one monthly plan at a time.

- Pilot pricing is limited to the first 3 clients or the first 90 days of validation.

- Do not discount Standard unless trading for a named case study, referral access, or annual prepay.

- Setup fee pays for implementation. Monthly fee pays for management, optimization, reporting, and support.

- Setup fee is split 50/50: 50% at signing, 50% at go-live. Setup fee becomes non-refundable 7 calendar days after signing. Monthly retainer auto-charges via Stripe starting at go-live (not at signing).

- Avoid first-month-free with no setup fee. It is too weak for cash flow and underfunds proper onboarding.

- Annual prepay can be offered at approximately 15% off monthly fees after the setup fee is paid. Mentioned only if asked.

- Custom work is scoped separately at a premium rate or declined.

| Scenario | Recommended structure |
| --- | --- |
| First 3 clients | $3,500 setup + $1,500/mo, 90-day minimum, case-study expectation. |
| Main standard offer | $5,500 setup + $2,000/mo, 90-day minimum, then month-to-month. |
| Premium / complex | $9,500 setup + $3,500/mo, with explicit scope and integration boundaries. |
| Annual prepay | Setup fee + 12 months prepaid at approximately 15% discount. |
| Customer asks for setup waived | No. Offer split payment if needed, not a waived setup. |
| Customer wants only audit | Offer paid audit or implementation sprint, but avoid becoming a one-off consulting shop. |

# 11. Compliance and Risk Controls

## Compliance principles

- Every SMS workflow must include appropriate consent handling, sender identification, opt-out language, and STOP handling.

- Do not rely casually on implied consent for marketing-style SMS campaigns. Treat express consent as the operational default.

- Quiet hours and permitted-hour rules must be respected for proactive messages.

- A2P 10DLC or equivalent registration/verification must be planned before messaging launches at scale.

- Client data must have retention, deletion, and access procedures aligned with Canadian privacy expectations.

- Do not design funnels around interest-based financing CTAs if the business is committed to halal-aligned positioning.

- Never make pricing promises, revenue guarantees, fake scarcity claims, fake reviews, or deceptive test inquiries.

## Operational guarantee

Recommended guarantee: If the core system is not live within 21 days, we continue working at no additional charge until it is live. If connected inquiries and flagged estimates are not being logged with source, status, and follow-up activity by day 30, we pause monthly billing until fixed.

## What not to guarantee

- Closed revenue.

- Exact number of jobs.

- Exact pipeline value.

- Specific close-rate lift.

- Ad performance.

- Customer sales ability or homeowner decision speed.

| Client has low lead volume | System cannot demonstrate value quickly. | Use qualification floor and volume-adjusted expectations. |
| Client does not flag estimates | Core automation underused. | Use SMS triggers, fallback nudges, and admin training. |
| Compliance delays | Go-live slips. | Start registration/verification early and use conservative launch timelines. |
| Attribution becomes messy | Delivery becomes technical rabbit hole. | Reserve deep attribution for Premium clients only. |
| Cold outreach underperforms | Pipeline stalls. | Use warmer channels, local events, paid audit, and tighter list filtering. |
| No proof yet | Price resistance. | Pilot tier for first 3 clients with case-study expectation. |
| Client expects revenue guarantee | Refund / reputation risk. | Use operational guarantee and explain what is outside control. |
