# 13 — Cold Outreach Batch 1

## What this is
Your first 25 personalized cold emails to Calgary/Edmonton design-build renovators. The Day 3 morning task. Volume is intentional — small enough to personalize each one, large enough to surface response patterns by lunch.

## Before you start this
- [ ] Your prospect list (file 11) has at least 30 qualified contractors with first name, business name, email, and one specific data point per row
- [ ] Your audit Loom template (file 10) is recorded and shareable
- [ ] You have read `docs/operations/COLD-START-PLAYBOOK.md` §The Scripts at least once

## Time required
~90 minutes (10–15 emails per hour for 25 emails)

## What you'll do

### Why a batch of 25
Large enough that subject-line and opener performance becomes statistically real (open + reply rates start to mean something). Small enough that you can personalize the first sentence of every single one. Anything bigger than 50 in one sitting and you start copy-pasting — and copy-paste reads as copy-paste.

### Pick one of three email variants per prospect

The three variants below map to the messaging patterns in `docs/operations/COLD-START-PLAYBOOK.md` §The Scripts (Scripts A, C, and D). Use whichever fits the data point you have on that prospect.

- **Email A — Specific Finding.** Lead with one number you found about their business vs a competitor. "I noticed [Business] has 12 Google reviews; [Competitor] has 84. I can tell you what that gap is likely costing you in 60 seconds."
- **Email B — Missed Call Hook.** Lead with the missed-call-recovery angle. "I built a tool that texts back missed calls in 5 seconds. I know you miss calls on job sites — want to see what I'd send back if it were your number?"
- **Email C — Direct Pain Point.** Lead with the dead-quote question (the single most effective opener from the contractor-empathy work). "Do you know which of your quoted jobs from the last 90 days you actually lost, or do they just go quiet and you never find out?"

### Personalization rule (non-negotiable)

Every email must cite ONE specific data point about THAT prospect. Pick from your prospect list:

- GBP review count vs a named competitor in their area
- Response time test (you texted their public number from a burner — what happened)
- Pain angle from the website ("your portfolio shows 14 basement projects, but I don't see a follow-up flow on your contact form")
- Project specialty ("you specialize in heritage homes in Inglewood")

If you do not have a real data point for a row, skip it. Do not send a generic email — that row goes in Batch 2 once you have the data point.

### Pacing

- 10–15 emails per hour. That is 4–6 minutes per email: 90 seconds to re-read the prospect row, 90 seconds to pick the variant + write the first sentence, 30 seconds to copy in the rest, 30 seconds to log it.
- 25 emails = ~90 min. If you find yourself going faster than 15/hour, you are not personalizing enough — slow down.

### Send timing

- Mornings, 9:00–11:00 AM local time (their local time — Calgary/Edmonton are MST/MDT; if you are in another zone, schedule via Gmail / your sender).
- Avoid Mondays before noon (cluttered inbox, weekend backlog). Tuesday through Thursday morning is the sweet spot.

### Track every send

In your prospect list spreadsheet, for each Batch 1 row, fill these columns:

- `sent_date_batch_1` — today
- `variant` — A, B, or C
- `data_point_used` — one phrase ("12 vs 84 reviews vs Trico")
- `opened` — fill in tomorrow morning when you check
- `replied` — fill in as replies arrive

This is the data you'll use this afternoon (file 15) to refine Batch 2.

### After Batch 1

Take a 60-minute break before Batch 2. Eat. Walk. Do not refresh your inbox. Email response data is most useful after letting it accumulate for an hour or two — refreshing every 5 minutes will not change your reply rate.

## What success looks like
- [ ] 25 personalized cold emails sent to 25 different prospects
- [ ] Every email cites one specific data point about that prospect
- [ ] Spreadsheet `sent_date_batch_1` and `variant` columns are filled for all 25 rows
- [ ] You took a 60-min break and are ready for Batch 2 in the afternoon

## If something goes wrong
- **You ran out of data points before 25 emails.** Send what you have, then go back to the prospect list (file 11) and spend the freed time adding data points to more rows. Quality > volume.
- **Gmail flagged you for sending too fast.** Slow down to 1 email every 5 minutes. Gmail throttles new senders to ~150/day; if you hit a soft block, wait 24h.
- **You realize a script doesn't sound like you.** Rewrite it once in your own voice. Use that version for the rest of the batch — but don't rewrite per email.
